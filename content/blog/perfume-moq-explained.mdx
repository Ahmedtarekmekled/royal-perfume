MOQ — minimum order quantity — is the smallest amount a supplier will sell at a given price. It sounds like a single number, but for wholesale fragrance it's almost always a tiered structure, and misunderstanding that is how first-time buyers end up either overpaying for a too-small order or overcommitting capital to a too-large one.

## MOQ is a pricing tier, not a gate

Most wholesale fragrance suppliers don't have one MOQ — they have several, each unlocking a different per-unit price. A 50-unit order and a 500-unit order aren't the same conversation with the same supplier at the same price; they're two different quotes entirely. Below the lowest tier, you're usually still welcome to order, just at standard per-unit pricing rather than wholesale rates. Above the highest tier, per-unit pricing typically keeps improving, since a larger order spreads the supplier's fixed handling and processing costs across more units.

This matters because it reframes the question. It's not "can I meet the MOQ" — it's "which tier makes sense for what I'm trying to test or fulfill right now."

## Why suppliers set MOQs in the first place

A wholesale operation isn't just a formula and a bottle. Behind every order sits warehousing, quality control, order processing, packing, and shipping coordination — fixed costs that don't shrink much whether the order is 20 units or 2,000. Below a certain volume, those fixed costs eat too much of the margin for wholesale pricing to make sense for the supplier. MOQ tiers are how suppliers translate that reality into a pricing structure buyers can actually plan around, rather than negotiating from scratch on every order.

## How to size your first order around MOQ tiers

MOQ tiers change the math on this directly: since a mixed 30-SKU order and a focused 5-SKU order rarely land in the same pricing tier, spreading a first order thin often means paying standard pricing on every SKU instead of wholesale pricing on a few — the tier structure itself rewards depth in a category you understand over breadth across ones you're guessing at.

A more reliable approach:

1. **Ask about MOQ tiers before you decide quantity**, not after. Your budget should follow the tier that fits your test, not the other way around.
2. **Pick 3-5 SKUs you'd genuinely bet on**, not 30 you're unsure about.
3. **Check whether mixed assortments qualify for the same tier as a single-scent order.** Some suppliers price mixed pallets differently than volume orders of one SKU — ask directly rather than assuming.
4. **Leave room to reorder fast** on whatever sells, instead of sinking the whole budget into one large first order.

## What changes above and below the threshold

Below the wholesale threshold, expect standard per-unit pricing with more flexibility on assortment and lower total capital required — useful for testing a new category or a new market before committing further. Above it, expect meaningfully better per-unit economics but less room to test broadly within a single order, since the pricing logic rewards depth in fewer SKUs over breadth across many.

Neither side is automatically the right move. A retailer testing a new fragrance category should usually start below the threshold; a retailer who already knows what sells in their market and is restocking should usually order at or above it.

## Questions worth asking any supplier about their MOQ structure

- What are the actual tier thresholds, and how does per-unit price change at each one?
- Does a mixed assortment qualify for the same tier as a single-SKU order?
- Is there flexibility to combine multiple smaller orders toward a higher tier over time?
- What happens if I reorder the same SKUs later — does my pricing tier carry over?

A supplier who can answer these specifically, rather than quoting one flat number, is pricing carefully and is worth taking seriously.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is MOQ the same for every fragrance in a catalog?**
Not necessarily. Some suppliers set a blanket MOQ across the whole catalog; others price certain SKUs or lines differently. Ask specifically rather than assuming a single number applies everywhere.

**What happens if I order below the MOQ?**
Most suppliers, including Royal Perfumes, will still fill smaller orders at standard per-unit pricing rather than refusing them outright — you just won't get wholesale-tier rates until you cross the threshold.

**Do mixed-scent orders count toward MOQ the same way as single-SKU orders?**
It depends on the supplier. Confirm this before you plan your order, since some price mixed assortments differently than volume orders of one fragrance.
