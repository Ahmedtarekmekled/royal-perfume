Perfume is one of the more complicated products to ship internationally, and it has nothing to do with the bottle. Most fragrances run at least 24% alcohol by volume, which classifies them as a flammable liquid for freight purposes. That single fact drives most of what makes perfume shipping different from ordinary cargo, and it's the first thing worth understanding before you place a wholesale order.

## Why perfume ships differently than other products

Because of the alcohol content, carriers apply specific handling requirements: limits on liquid volume per package, particular sealing methods, and mandatory hazard labeling on the outer packaging. Shipments that skip these steps get held at customs or refused by the carrier entirely, which is a far more common cause of delay than anything related to price or demand.

The second factor is documentation. Customs authorities want a clear, accurate description of what's in the shipment: product names, sizes, alcohol content, and country of manufacture. Missing or vague paperwork is the single biggest cause of customs delays for first-time importers, more so than the product itself.

## What to have ready before you order

- **Your business's import registration**, if your country requires one for cosmetics or fragrance products.
- **A clear destination address and contact**, since incomplete address data is a common cause of clearance holds.
- **An understanding of your local duty treatment.** Many countries classify fragrance under an international harmonized system (HS) code specific to perfumes, but the actual duty rate applied varies significantly by country and trade agreement — it's not a single global number, so it's worth confirming with your own customs broker or authority rather than assuming a rate.

## Questions to ask your supplier before you pay

1. **Do you handle export documentation, or is that entirely on me?** A supplier experienced in international wholesale should be able to prepare accurate commercial invoices and product descriptions on request.
2. **Is the shipping cost confirmed before I pay, or estimated?** Freight pricing for flammable-liquid cargo varies a lot by destination and carrier — a quote that isn't destination-specific isn't a real quote yet.
3. **What happens if customs holds the shipment?** Ask this before it happens, not after.
4. **What's the realistic delivery window for my country?**, including customs clearance time, not just transit time.
